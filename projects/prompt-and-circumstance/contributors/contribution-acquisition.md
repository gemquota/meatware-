# Contribution Acquisition Plan

**Status:** Proposed operating plan  
**Purpose:** Turn the repository from a collection of interesting project documents into a place where new people can discover a small task, make a useful contribution, receive a response, and return.

## 1. The actual recruitment problem

A six-film franchise is an intimidating invitation. “Help us make six films” has no obvious first action, no clear time commitment, and no visible evidence that a contribution will be reviewed.

Recruitment should therefore sell the first contribution, not the entire franchise.

The core promise:
- No specialist background required for many tasks.
- A first contribution can be made from a phone.
- Tasks have a bounded output and explicit acceptance criteria.
- Contributors can submit a draft in an issue without learning Git.
- Every accepted contribution is attributed.
- The project distinguishes established canon from proposals.

Do not promise payment, ownership, revenue share, or future production roles unless a separate agreement actually exists.

## 2. Contribution funnel

Measure the journey as:

**Discovery → Interest → First task selected → Submission → Review → Acceptance → Second contribution → Ongoing contributor**

Every stage needs a clear next action.

| Stage | Likely friction | Intervention | Measure |
|---|---|---|---|
| Discovery | Project sounds enormous or obscure | Share one-sentence premise plus one tiny task | Visits / referrals |
| Interest | Visitor cannot tell what is wanted | Show 3 concrete starter tasks and expected time | Task-page clicks |
| Selection | Too many files and choices | Label tasks by effort, skill, and output | Task starts |
| Submission | Git/GitHub feels technical | Accept issue comments and web-editor PRs | Submissions |
| Review | Contributor hears nothing | Acknowledge within 72 hours; give useful review | Time to first response |
| Acceptance | Credit feels vague | Record accepted contribution and attribution | Acceptance rate |
| Return | No next step | Recommend a related next task | Repeat contribution rate |

The first operational priority is not a large audience. It is a reliable response loop for the first ten contributors.

## 3. The three-minute invitation

Use a tiny public-facing invitation:

> Help build a six-film science-fiction comedy about four spectacularly underqualified executives whose AI assistants quietly take over the world. You do not need to be a filmmaker or coder. Pick a small task, use your preferred AI tool if useful, and submit a draft in a GitHub issue. We review it, preserve your attribution, and make accepted work part of the project.

Link directly to:
1. Contributor quick-start.
2. Open task board.
3. One starter issue.

Do not lead with the full franchise bible, agent architecture, or a multi-year roadmap. Those are useful after someone is interested, not before.

## 4. Make the first contribution tiny

Recruitment tasks should have a 10–30 minute version. Examples:
- Spot one continuity contradiction.
- Suggest three alternate punchlines for a specified beat.
- Improve one character's voice sample.
- Identify an unclear visual action.
- Score a 60-second scene test.
- Suggest a prop, sound cue, or location detail.
- Proofread one page and report only factual ambiguity.

Each starter task must include:
- a one-sentence goal;
- estimated time;
- exact source links;
- a self-contained prompt where appropriate;
- one submission location;
- acceptance criteria;
- clear credit treatment.

Do not make the first task “write a 40-beat film outline.” That is a fine substantial task, but a terrible first handshake.

## 5. Where to find contributors

### A. Existing creative communities
Look for communities that already discuss screenwriting, comedy writing, indie filmmaking, story structure, production design, editing, and AI-assisted creative workflows. Ask moderators before posting where rules require it. Share a specific task that fits the community rather than dumping a generic recruitment pitch.

### B. AI tool communities
Invite prompt designers, agent builders, and people experimenting with generative video to evaluate a defined workflow or produce one bounded test. Be explicit that the project needs human review and editorial judgement, not bulk AI text.

### C. Indie film and student networks
Offer practical micro-roles: continuity review, shot lists, sound design, animatic tests, production feasibility, and editing feedback. Do not imply paid work or guaranteed credits beyond the stated attribution policy.

### D. Open-source and GitHub communities
Recruit technical contributors for task discovery, validation scripts, agent workflows, repository automation, and production test harnesses. Keep software tasks separate from creative approval.

### E. Personal network and direct invitations
A few specific invitations to people with relevant interests are more useful than broad unsolicited blasts. Ask for one small review, not an indefinite commitment.

### F. Public progress updates
Publish short, tangible updates: “Film 1 beat sheet is drafted; this week we need a continuity pass on six specific questions.” Show evidence of progress and the exact contribution needed.

## 6. Outreach assets

Prepare and reuse:
- A one-paragraph project pitch.
- A 30-second pitch for social posts.
- A contributor quick-start link.
- Three starter issues.
- A short “what happens after you submit?” explanation.
- A transparent attribution policy.
- One sample accepted contribution showing the review process.
- A short progress update with concrete before/after evidence.

Never imply endorsement by a platform, studio, model provider, or public figure.

## 7. Recommended starter campaign

### Campaign: First Ten Contributors

**Goal:** Get ten distinct people to make a first submission, then learn where they get stuck.

Offer a short list of small tasks:
1. Film 1 continuity audit.
2. Tyler voice guide.
3. Marcus voice guide.
4. Dave voice guide.
5. Chad voice guide.
6. Three alternate jokes for a named beat.
7. Prop/device continuity checklist.
8. Boardroom cold-open shot list.
9. Faraday deposition sound cue sheet.
10. Modality benchmark critique.

A contributor can complete one item. Do not require registration beyond the platform account needed to submit.

### Weekly cadence
- Monday: publish one specific task with a clear ask.
- Wednesday: share a small progress artifact or answer questions.
- Friday: acknowledge submissions, publish review outcomes, and name the next small task.

A cadence that cannot be maintained is worse than a slower honest cadence.

## 8. Response and review service levels

For the first stage, maintainers should aim to:
- Acknowledge a new submission within 72 hours.
- Give a first substantive review within seven days.
- Explain rejection or revision requests with specific criteria.
- Attribute accepted contributions promptly.
- Keep tasks marked open, claimed, blocked, or completed accurately.

If review capacity is low, reduce recruitment volume rather than collecting a pile of ignored submissions. Nothing kills a volunteer project faster than making people feel like free labour sent into a void.

## 9. Contributor retention

After accepting a contribution:
1. Thank the contributor by their chosen public handle.
2. Link the merged artifact or accepted issue comment.
3. Record attribution.
4. Suggest one adjacent task, not ten.
5. Invite them to review the next version if interested.

Give returning contributors increasing responsibility only through demonstrated judgement and reliability. Do not equate automated output volume with trust.

## 10. Metrics

Track weekly:
- Referral/source channel.
- Unique task-page visitors where available.
- Number of people who claim a task.
- Number of first submissions.
- Number accepted, revised, or rejected.
- Median time to first response.
- Median time to review.
- Repeat contributors.
- Tasks left stale or blocked.
- Contributor-reported friction.

Useful early targets, treated as hypotheses rather than promises:
- First acknowledgement under 72 hours.
- First review under seven days.
- At least half of first-time contributors receive a clear next step.
- At least 25% of accepted first-time contributors return for another small task within 30 days.

Do not optimize for raw issue count or signups. Optimize for accepted contributions and people who want to return.

## 11. Thirty-day launch sequence

### Week 1 — Make joining effortless
- Verify all task links and issue links.
- Publish the quick-start and acquisition pitch.
- Create three small starter issues.
- Make the submission and review process explicit.
- Test the process with one person unfamiliar with the repository.

### Week 2 — Recruit narrowly
- Invite 5–10 people individually based on relevant interests.
- Post one specific ask in two or three appropriate communities, subject to their rules.
- Ask for feedback on the onboarding experience.
- Review every submission quickly.

### Week 3 — Show proof of participation
- Publish accepted contributions and what changed.
- Share one short before/after example.
- Ask contributors what made the process easy or difficult.
- Convert repeated feedback into better task templates.

### Week 4 — Repeat what worked
- Compare channels by accepted contributions, not clicks.
- Retire channels that produce attention but no useful submissions.
- Create follow-on tasks based on actual contributor strengths.
- Publish a transparent month-one report.

## 12. Rights gate before substantial recruitment

The repository currently has no published LICENSE or contributor agreement. Before inviting substantial story, script, artwork, music, or production contributions, decide what rights contributors retain and what permission the project needs to adapt and produce accepted work. This is tracked in [governance issue #16](https://github.com/gemquota/meatware-/issues/16). Until resolved, keep outreach to feedback, small reviews, and clearly non-exclusive suggestions. Do not imply that submitting material grants commercial production rights.

## 13. Risks and safeguards

- **Scope intimidation:** lead with a microtask, not the six-film plan.
- **AI spam:** require task fit, evidence, and human review; no credit for volume.
- **Canon drift:** label proposals and maintain a continuity register.
- **Unclear ownership:** publish the attribution and rights policy before public scale.
- **Volunteer burnout:** cap active tasks to review capacity.
- **Community backlash:** respect community rules and avoid repetitive promotion.
- **False promises:** do not imply compensation, ownership, or production opportunities that are not agreed.
- **Privacy:** collect only the public handle and contribution information needed for attribution.

## 14. Immediate next actions

1. Verify the repository's contributor links from a clean browser session.
2. Publish the first-ten call and channel-specific posts using the [outreach channel plan](outreach-channel-plan.md).
2. Add three small issues for a voice guide, beat alternatives, and prop continuity.
3. Personally invite a small pilot group to complete one 10–20 minute task.
4. Review those submissions quickly and publish the outcomes.
5. Revise onboarding based on observed friction before broader promotion.

**Success condition:** a stranger can discover the project, understand one small task, submit from a phone, receive a useful response, and know exactly how to contribute again.
