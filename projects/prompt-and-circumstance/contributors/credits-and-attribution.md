# Credits and Attribution Policy

**Status:** Proposed policy for maintainer approval. No contributor is promised payment, ownership, revenue share, or a particular credit position by this document.

## Principles
1. Credit accepted work, not activity volume.
2. Preserve an auditable record of who contributed what.
3. Do not reward unreviewed AI output or duplicate submissions.
4. A human contributor remains attributable when using an AI tool.
5. Credit corrections should be explicit adjustment records, not silent history rewrites.
6. Recognition must not imply copyright ownership, employment, or financial entitlement unless a separate written agreement says so.

## Proposed unit model
- A task declares a base credit weight before work begins.
- A normal accepted contribution earns the task's base weight.
- Technical automation may earn a higher weight only when it demonstrably adds validation, reproducibility, or integration work beyond producing more text.
- The source proposal of Tier 1 = 1.0× and Tier 2 = 3.0× is retained as a proposal for discussion, not an automatic rule.
- Reviewers may split credit among multiple contributors based on documented contributions.
- Rejected, duplicate, or materially unusable submissions receive no accepted-work credit, though useful review feedback may be recognized separately.

## Review states
proposed → in_review → accepted or rejected. A revision request remains attached to the original contribution. A contributor may resubmit a revised version.

## Attribution record
The machine-readable record should include stable contribution ID, contributor handle or chosen public attribution, task ID, artifact path, issue/PR URL and number, contribution type, status, base weight and accepted weight, reviewer, review timestamp, tool disclosure when supplied, and adjustment or supersession reference if any.

Do not store email addresses, legal names, or other personal data unless the contributor explicitly requests it and there is a clear need.

## Agent and multi-person work
- Name the human operator or team responsible for an agent submission.
- Disclose autonomous or agent-assisted work.
- Credit the human who directs, reviews, and accepts responsibility for the submission.
- Do not create artificial contributor identities for agents to inflate recognition.
- Multiple people may receive split credit when their roles are documented.

## Disputes and corrections
Raise a private or public issue depending on sensitivity. Maintainers should record the decision and add an adjustment entry. Never delete an accepted contributor's historical attribution merely because a later version replaces their text.

## Before public launch
The maintainer must decide whether credits are purely honorary or tied to any future benefit; the exact multiplier policy; whether credits appear in releases, a website, or both; how anonymous contributors are represented; and who can approve and amend records.

Until then, the only promise is accurate attribution for accepted contributions.
