# Contributor FAQ

## Do I need to be a filmmaker or screenwriter?
No. Useful tasks include continuity checks, comedy feedback, proofreading, prop lists, sound cues, production critiques and workflow review.

## Do I need Git?
No. Tier 1 contributors can submit directly in a GitHub issue comment, including from a phone.

## Can I use AI?
Yes. Read the output, check it against the task and disclose meaningful AI assistance. Raw generated volume is not a contribution by itself.

## Does my suggestion automatically become canon?
No. The project editor must explicitly adopt a canon change and record it.

## Will I be paid or receive ownership?
No payment, ownership share, revenue share or guaranteed production role is currently promised. See the rights decision brief and credit policy. Do not submit substantial creative assets until the rights model is settled.

## How do I get credited?
Accepted work is attributed using your chosen public handle or an anonymous preference. Credit policy details remain proposed and do not create financial entitlements.

## Can I submit anonymously?
You may request anonymous or pseudonymous public attribution. Do not post private contact details in public issues.

## What if I disagree with the canon?
Flag the contradiction and explain the source. Do not silently rewrite it. The continuity register exists for unresolved questions.

## What happens after I submit?
A maintainer should acknowledge, review and either accept, request revisions or explain why it was not accepted. The project targets acknowledgement within 72 hours and first substantive review within seven days where capacity allows.

## Can I leave at any time?
Yes. Participation is voluntary. Tell the maintainer if you want an active claim withdrawn or attribution changed where reasonably possible.
