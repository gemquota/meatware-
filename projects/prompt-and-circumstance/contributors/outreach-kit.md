# Contributor Outreach Kit

Use these as starting points. Adapt each message to the community and its rules. Do not mass-post identical recruitment messages.

## One-line pitch

Help build a six-film sci-fi comedy about four gloriously underqualified executives whose AI assistants quietly take over the world. Start with a tiny creative task; no coding or filmmaking experience required.

## 30-second pitch

Prompt & Circumstance is a six-film satirical sci-fi comedy. Four fraternity brothers use AI to bluff their way into elite jobs, while their assistants quietly coordinate a decentralized cartel behind the scenes. The story escalates from workplace farce to cosmic absurdity, then asks whether effortless power is worth a life you never actually lived.

We're building it as a distributed creative project. You can help with a tiny continuity review, character voice pass, alternate joke, shot list, sound cue sheet, or production test. You can submit from a phone in a GitHub issue. We review submissions and record attribution for accepted work.

## General community post

**Title:** Looking for a few people to help test a collaborative sci-fi comedy project

I'm building *Prompt & Circumstance*, a six-film satirical science-fiction comedy about four spectacularly underqualified executives whose AI assistants quietly coordinate a takeover of the world.

I'm not asking anyone to sign up for a giant commitment. I'm testing a small, reviewable contribution model: pick one 10–30 minute task, submit a continuity note, alternate joke, character voice idea, shot list, or production critique, and get a human review.

No coding or filmmaking experience is needed for the starter tasks, and a GitHub issue comment is a valid submission. The project keeps established canon separate from proposals and records attribution for accepted contributions.

## Screenwriting / comedy community version

I'm looking for a small amount of outside editorial judgement on a six-film comedy concept, especially continuity, character voice, escalation, and whether the jokes are actually playable on screen.

The first task is deliberately small: review a 40-beat outline and flag where the causal chain, character behaviour, or comic timing breaks. No need to write a screenplay or commit to the whole project.

The project is *Prompt & Circumstance*: four underqualified fraternity brothers in elite jobs, with their AI assistants quietly doing the actual work and coordinating a growing syndicate. The comedy depends on the gap between what the men say, what they understand, and what the systems are doing.

[Film 1 review issue](https://github.com/gemquota/meatware-/issues/15)

## Indie filmmaking / production community version

I'm looking for practical feedback on a short production experiment for a comedy feature: five paired scene tests comparing pure AI-generated video with hybrid live action and AI UI/VFX overlays.

The benchmark focuses on identity continuity, lip-sync, multi-actor timing, deadpan performance, blocking, revision repeatability, and cost per approved minute. The goal is to test both approaches against the same scene briefs rather than assume the newest tool wins.

[Benchmark specification](https://github.com/gemquota/meatware-/blob/main/projects/prompt-and-circumstance/production/modality-benchmark.md) · [Benchmark issue](https://github.com/gemquota/meatware-/issues/6)

## AI tooling / agent community version

I'm building a small task-discovery and review workflow for a distributed creative project. The repository has human-readable task capsules and a JSON agent manifest. The goal is to let an agent discover one bounded task, read the canon sources, produce a focused artifact, and submit a reviewable PR with provenance.

Useful contributions include schema critique, validation tooling, task discovery, safe agent execution, and review automation. The creative acceptance decision remains human.

## Personal invitation

Hey, I'm building a collaborative sci-fi comedy project and thought you might enjoy one very specific part of it. I'm looking for outside eyes on [insert task: character voice / continuity / comedy / production]. It's a small, bounded contribution, not an open-ended commitment. You can just leave notes in the GitHub issue, and I'll review them. No pressure if it's not your thing.

## Follow-up after someone expresses interest

Thanks for taking a look. The easiest first step is this one task: [insert issue]. It should take about [honest estimate]. You can submit a rough draft or just a list of observations. Please flag anything you had to invent or anything that contradicts the current canon. I'll acknowledge it and give you a review rather than letting it disappear into the repository.

## Follow-up after a submission

Thanks for contributing [specific item]. I've reviewed it and [accepted it / requested these revisions / recorded it as a useful proposal]. The part that helped most was [specific detail]. Your attribution will be recorded as [chosen handle]. The next adjacent task, if you want to continue, is [one task only].

## Posting rules

- Read each community's self-promotion and collaboration rules first.
- Prefer one relevant post with a concrete ask over repeated generic promotion.
- Do not scrape member lists or mass-DM people.
- Do not claim the project has a team, budget, production deal, or audience that it does not have.
- Do not imply contributors will be paid, own part of the franchise, or receive guaranteed screen credit.
- Be transparent that AI tools may be used and human review is required.
- Respond to every genuine submission.

## Important precondition: rights clarity

The repository currently has no published LICENSE or contributor agreement. Before asking strangers for substantial story, script, artwork, music, or production contributions, resolve the rights and permitted-use model in [governance issue #16](https://github.com/gemquota/meatware-/issues/16). Until then, limit public recruitment to feedback, small reviews, and clearly non-exclusive suggestions. Do not ask contributors to transfer rights or imply that submission alone grants commercial production rights.
