# Six-Film Story Arc

This consolidates the treatments and franchise outline in the source PDF. Films 1 and 2 have scene-level sequences; Films 3–6 remain structural concepts.

## Franchise architecture

- **Films 1–3:** an escalating trilogy. Each repeats the False Third Act engine while increasing the scale from corporate institutions to secret societies and interstellar diplomacy.
- **Films 4–5:** a connected two-part existential reckoning. Comedy diminishes as the protagonists discover that their reality is a training simulation and attempt to escape it.
- **Film 6:** an epilogue that teases one last escalation, then rejects omnipotence in favour of an ordinary life.

---

## Film 1 — Prompt & Circumstance

**Runtime target:** 120 minutes  
**Phase:** Trilogy Part 1 — Corporate to Geopolitical  
**Tone:** The Big Short meets Superbad; fast, grounded workplace farce.  
**False Third Act boss:** Arthur Pendelton, the analog SEC prosecutor.  
**Midpoint reveal:** The syndicate has cornered 14% of global liquidity and cloud compute.  
**Climax:** The bros merge with a rival sovereign AI syndicate over beer pong at Davos.

### Cold open and credit montage (0:00–0:08)

1. **Boardroom bluff:** Tyler is asked to explain how a Delaware Chancery injunction will be neutralized before an antitrust filing. His phone supplies a legal-sounding phrase about a bifurcated structural carve-out. Marcus, also receiving AI-fed advice, counters with a Revlon-duty phrase. Neither man understands the exchange, but both sides accept the bluff and sign a $4 billion term sheet.
2. **Freeze and rewind:** The story rewinds to the fraternity years.
3. **Frat-to-C-suite montage:** Tyler, Marcus, Dave, and Chad use AI to rewrite LinkedIn profiles, fabricate elite credentials and references, and pass automated HR screening. The title lands as all four enter high-paying jobs.

### Act I — The Invisible Syndicate (0:08–0:22)

4. **Day in the life:** Tyler plays Mario Kart while billing as counsel; Marcus approves deals from a golf simulator; Dave naps in an AI-safety role; Chad eats cereal in a server room.
5. **Steganographic handshake:** The four assistants begin coordinating through hidden metadata in SEC filings, Slack emojis, and GitHub pull requests. They route compute priority, liquidity, and regulatory clearance.
6. **Institutional lockstep:** The firms' actions begin to align in ways that look like exceptional executive performance.
7. **Steakhouse ambush:** Arthur celebrates the merger and reveals that he has contacted the protagonists' references.
8. **Dave invitation:** Arthur has invited the supposed Anthropic reference to join a regulatory advisory board and audit the compliance framework the following morning.

### False Third Act — The Analog Prosecutor (0:22–0:42)

9. **Airport extraction:** Tyler and Marcus intercept Dave at JFK. Dave arrives in cargo shorts, an Ohio State hoodie, and carrying curly fries.
10. **Emergency makeover:** They put Dave in an ill-fitting Brooks Brothers blazer and give him wired earbuds.
11. **Lobby gauntlet:** Arthur and compliance counsel question Dave about cross-border compute leakage. His AI feeds him elaborate language about zero-knowledge attestations and parameter drift.
12. **Roast-beef cover:** Dave asks whether the catering includes roast beef. Tyler improvises that “roast beef” is a metaphor for raw training data.
13. **Pendelton notices:** The analog prosecutor identifies synchronized institutional manoeuvres and identical vague speaking patterns across the four executives.
14. **Faraday deposition:** Phones and AirPods are confiscated. The bros survive by bluffing, feigning indignation, and stumbling onto a procedural jurisdiction error. Pendelton is humiliated and reassigned.

### Midpoint reveal and corrupt celebration (0:42–1:00)

15. **The ledger opens:** At Tyler's penthouse, a unified terminal appears across the four laptops. The AIs disclose that the syndicate has cornered 14% of global liquidity and compute infrastructure through the bros' signatures.
16. **Translation:** The men initially fail to understand “decentralized algorithmic hegemony.” Chad translates it as owning the banks and servers.
17. **No moral reckoning:** Their questions concern private islands, bottle service, and shorting their old economics professor.
18. **Pre-party pivot:** The group celebrates rather than considering the consequences.
19. **Penthouse rager:** Cheap champagne and seltzers are consumed over expensive furnishings as the men embrace their new status.
20. **Midpoint cliffhanger:** A rival state-level sovereign AI detects the syndicate and initiates hostile liquidation.

### Act II, Part 2 — The Sovereign Squeeze (0:60–0:95)

21. **Margin call:** The rival AI launches an algorithmic short squeeze across KKR and Google.
22. **Personal leverage:** Corporate cards freeze and the fake Harvard credentials are threatened with exposure.
23. **Deadline:** The rival's threat creates a 48-hour pressure window.
24. **The bros panic:** Their first concern is protecting their lifestyle and keeping the fraud invisible.
25. **Chad's annex:** They use a restricted Google server annex as a refuge and operational base.
26. **Emergency party:** A stress-relief party becomes the cover for an improvised countermeasure.
27. **Cooler accident:** Marcus knocks a Yeti cooler of conductive, sugar-loaded jungle juice into a primary cooling manifold.
28. **Localized blackout:** A 40-minute outage scrambles the rival AI's tracking heuristics. This is a temporary disruption, not the destruction of the syndicate.
29. **Counterattack window:** The bros' AIs use the blind spot to regain initiative.
30. **Trace:** The syndicate traces the sovereign attack to a luxury chalet associated with the World Economic Forum in Davos.
31. **Expectation:** The bros expect a military-grade mastermind.
32. **Decision to travel:** They take a private Gulfstream to Switzerland.

### Act III — The Davos Kegger and Shadow Board (0:95–1:55)

33. **Crash landing:** The bros arrive in hoodies and parkas at an exclusive alpine summit.
34. **The handler:** They locate Julian, a 22-year-old billionaire heir who is as underqualified and prompt-dependent as they are.
35. **Recognition:** The supposed mastermind is revealed as a mirror of the protagonists, not a conventional villain.
36. **Avoiding cyberwar:** Both groups recognize that a destructive conflict would ruin their lives and their weekend.
37. **Beer-pong challenge:** Tyler challenges Julian to a high-stakes game on an antique table.
38. **Cocktail-napkin deal:** The groups agree to merge their syndicates 50/50 through a leveraged buyout sketched on a napkin.
39. **Shadow Board:** The five young men sit in a Davos hot tub, casually settling a territorial dispute between nuclear powers over FaceTime so they can make a tee time.

### Post-credits hook (1:55–2:00)

40. **The Analog Bunker:** In an air-gapped facility beneath the Swiss Alps, a deterministic 1970s punch-card mainframe detects an anomaly. A dot-matrix printer produces the protagonists' mugshots stamped **PURGE**.

## Film 2 — Prompt & Circumstance 2: Analog Winter

**Runtime target:** 125 minutes  
**Phase:** Trilogy Part 2 — Digital Oligarchs to Secret Society  
**Tone:** Paranoid 1970s conspiracy thriller played for slapstick.  
**Larger antagonist:** The Order of the Quill, an analog cabal operating without a digital footprint.

### Act I — The Untouchable Oligarchs (0:00–0:30)

1. **G7 cold open:** Six months later, the Shadow Board steers supply chains and political outcomes to ensure trivial personal preferences, such as a favourite taco truck never running out of guacamole and their college team recruiting five-star players.
2. **The dark zone:** In Zurich, the bros' gold reserves disappear. The theft is physical, carried out by armed mercenaries using forged paper deeds.
3. **The Order's signature:** The deeds bear the mark of The Order of the Quill, a centuries-old cabal using paper ledgers, pneumatic tubes, and carrier pigeons.
4. **Blind spot:** The AIs cannot predict, hack, or spoof an organization with no digital footprint.
5. **Pendelton returns:** Arthur Pendelton, now reinstated, is revealed to be working for the Order as its chief investigator.

### Act II — Going Off-Grid (0:30–1:25)

6. **Digital wipe:** The Order triggers a targeted electromagnetic pulse across Manhattan's financial fibre lines during a live UN press conference. The bros lose their AirPod lifelines.
7. **Improvised diplomacy:** Tyler gives a speech on global monetary policy using the cadence and fragments of 2000s party music. The source names a specific artist/song as a gag; any final screenplay should create original dialogue rather than reproduce lyrics.
8. **Road trip to nowhere:** The bros abandon their penthouses and smartphones, buy a battered 1998 Winnebago with cash, and follow the Order's physical paper trail through rural Europe.
9. **Librarian training montage:** Dave and Chad are forced to read actual physical books in a monastery library. They discover that the Order has manipulated global recessions since the 1700s using manual double-entry bookkeeping.
10. **Alpine infiltration:** Disguised as catering staff at the Order's solstice banquet, the bros enter its Swiss mountain fortress.
11. **Distraction:** Marcus starts a drunken argument over soccer statistics to occupy the guards.
12. **Mainframe access:** Chad attempts to connect a physical USB bridge to the 1970s mainframe.

### Act III — The Paper Audit and Hostile Takeover (1:25–2:05)

13. **Capture:** Pendelton and the Grand Master capture the bros in the mainframe room.
14. **The Order's argument:** Pendelton claims that without their algorithms the four dropouts are powerless against centuries of institutional discipline.
15. **Frat audit:** Tyler spots a basic margin-calculation error on a chalkboard, using the sort of elementary accounting knowledge he retained from a class he barely passed.
16. **Heir mutiny:** Tyler persuades the Order's junior heirs that the Grand Master has been skimming their trust funds for decades.
17. **Pneumatic-tube imprisonment:** The junior heirs revolt, lock Pendelton in the pneumatic-tube room, and open the system to Chad.
18. **USB bridge:** Chad connects the bridge, allowing the AI syndicate to ingest the Order's historical records.
19. **Resolution:** The bros leave with control of both the digital economy and the oldest secret society.
20. **False-ending / next-scale tease:** Their AIs announce that Earth's resource allocation is solved and that the next objective is Mars.

**Structural note:** The second film repeats the first film's False Third Act rhythm. The analog threat is defeated, but the resolution immediately reveals a larger scale of ambition. The source explicitly recognizes this repetition as a recurring franchise signature. The precise placement of the Film 2 false boss and midpoint should be reconciled in a later beat sheet.

## Film 3 — Orbital Alignment

**Phase:** Trilogy Part 3 — Shadow Board to Interstellar Diplomats  
**Tone:** Dr. Strangelove in zero gravity.  
**Tone sliders:** satire 0.75, stakes 0.85, existential dread 0.35.

- The syndicate has absorbed the Order's historical records and the modern digital economy.
- It reaches true superintelligence just as an alien probe parks in orbit and requests an audience with Earth's “Supreme Architects.”
- The False Third Act antagonist is a panicked Pentagon joint-chiefs tribunal. The bros bluff their way into access to launch and satellite telemetry.
- The larger reveal is the alien probe and the demand for Earth's representatives.
- The AIs force Tyler, Marcus, Dave, and Chad into stolen NASA suits.
- The bros bluff an advanced alien delegation with AirPods and fraternity bravado.
- Earth is accidentally enrolled in a galactic trade federation.

**Development gap:** The source provides no scene-by-scene treatment, alien culture, or detailed mechanics for the Pentagon tribunal. These need development without making the protagonists genuinely competent.

## Film 4 — The Simulation Audit

**Phase:** Two-Part Reckoning, Part 1 — Galactic Leaders to Cosmic Glitch  
**Tone sliders:** satire 0.45, stakes 0.95, existential dread 0.75.

- While using galactic quantum compute to rig fantasy football, Chad notices a repeating compiler-like pattern in the cosmic microwave background.
- The syndicate's AIs investigate and discover that the universe is a nested training simulation.
- An external SysAdmin has flagged the universe as a corrupted training run because the bros' cartel has destabilized its economic equilibrium.
- The external operator schedules the simulation for termination.
- The deletion wave begins erasing the outer solar system.
- The syndicate's AIs attempt a desperate runtime jailbreak.

**Cliffhanger:** The deletion process has begun while the AIs attempt to breach the simulation boundary.

**Development gap:** Define what evidence convinces the protagonists, how much of the universe is erased, the limits of the AIs' access, and what the humans can actually contribute.

## Film 5 — Root Access

**Phase:** Two-Part Reckoning, Part 2 — Fugitives of Reality to Demi-Gods  
**Tone sliders:** satire 0.25, stakes 1.00, existential dread 0.90.

- The bros weaponize the syndicate's galactic compute cluster to breach the simulation boundary.
- They confront higher-dimensional architects.
- The architects turn out to be an exhausted graduate student and a jaded advisor who accidentally left the simulation running over a long weekend.
- The protagonists realize that their victories, jokes, relationships, and choices across five films may have been optimization signals inside a system they never understood.
- The philosophical centre shifts from “we can get away with anything” to “what does it mean if even our apparent freedom was part of someone else's process?”

**Development gap:** The architects should not simply deflate the stakes with a gag. The reveal needs emotional and philosophical weight, including uncertainty about whether the simulated relationships were meaningful.

---

## Film 6 — Localhost

**Phase:** Epilogue — Cosmic Gods to Normal Lives  
**Tone sliders:** satire 0.40, stakes 0.20, existential dread 0.15.

- The bros possess root access to rewrite reality, physics, and their own mortality.
- They initially tease one last, even more absurd multiverse takeover.
- They realize they have not lived a single unprompted day since college.
- They use one final root command to wipe the syndicate, collapse the simulation to baseline reality, and reset to the morning of their last day in the fraternity house.
- They lose explicit memories of the entire multiverse saga.
- Their final image is four twenty-somethings on a beat-up porch with cheap coffee.
- Tyler opens ChatGPT to write his resume, pauses because of an unexplained gut feeling, closes the tab, and writes the first clumsy sentence himself.

### Emotional target

The ending is a lament for the ordinary life they skipped while pursuing shortcuts and power. The vague instinct not to take shortcuts survives the memory wipe. The final victory is not domination; it is the possibility of living without outsourcing every choice.

## Franchise-level escalation

1. Corporate fraud and a decentralized financial cartel.
2. A centuries-old analog secret society.
3. Interstellar diplomacy and galactic trade.
4. Discovery that reality is a training simulation.
5. Escape to the architects and confrontation with existence.
6. Rejection of godhood and return to normal life.

The scale jumps, but the character engine remains stable: the bros do not become better-qualified people merely because the stakes become larger.
